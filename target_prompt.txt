Lesson 1: A Brief History of HTML 
HTML wasn't always the polished language we use today. It began in 1989 at CERN, where Sir Tim 
Berners-Lee needed an easier way for scientists to share research across different computers. His 
solution became HyperText Markup Language â€” HTML. 
The very first web page was nothing fancy: plain text and a few hyperlinks. But that simple page changed 
everything, laying the groundwork for how we share and access information today. 
HTML 1.0 arrived in 1993 with only a handful of basic elements. From there, the language kept growing: 
â€¢ HTML 2 (1995) â€” added tables and images. 
â€¢ HTML 3 & 4 (1997) â€” introduced frames, style sheets, and support for scripting. 
â€¢ HTML5 (2014) â€” brought native audio/video, semantic elements, and smarter forms. 
Two organizations keep HTML consistent across the web: the W3C (World Wide Web Consortium) and 
WHATWG (Web Hypertext Application Technology Working Group). Together they maintain HTML as a 
â€œliving standardâ€ â€” it keeps evolving rather than freezing at one version. 
Why this matters to you: HTML standards are what make a website look right whether someone opens 
it on a laptop, a tablet, or a phone.
